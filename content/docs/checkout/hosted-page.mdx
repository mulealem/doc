# Hosted checkout page

The hosted checkout page is the surface your customer actually sees. It lives on a separate origin (`checkout.example.com`) from your dashboard and your app, and is fully managed by PyGate.

## Flow

```
customer clicks "Pay" on your site
  → you POST /api/v1/orders
  → you receive { paymentUrl, ... }
  → you redirect customer to paymentUrl
  → customer sees hosted page
  → customer picks bank / telebirr
  → customer pays from their bank app
  → customer returns to hosted page
  → customer uploads a screenshot of the receipt
  → hosted page polls for verification
  → on success: redirect to your successUrl
  → on failure: redirect to your cancelUrl
```

## URL

```
https://checkout.example.com/pay/{orderId}
```

The `orderId` is the `orderId` returned by `POST /api/v1/orders`. Don't construct the URL yourself — use the `paymentUrl` field verbatim.

## Customizing the page

By default, the page shows:

- Your project logo (uploaded in the dashboard)
- The order description and amount
- A list of enabled bank accounts the customer can pay into
- A receipt upload widget after the customer pays

You can customize:

- **Logo** — Project → Settings → Logo (PNG, ≤ 500 KB)
- **Accent color** — Project → Settings → Brand
- **Receipt widget language** — localized to English, Amharic, Afaan Oromoo, and Somali
- **Bank account order** — drag to reorder in the dashboard

## Query parameters

You can append optional query parameters to the `paymentUrl`:

| Param | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `?lang=am` | Force UI language (auto-detected from browser by default) |
| `?theme=dark` | Force dark mode regardless of system preference |

## Success and cancel URLs

The hosted page does not currently support merchant-return URLs (planned for v1.5). Until then, detect success by:

- Receiving a `payment.approved` webhook, or
- Polling `GET /api/v1/orders/{orderId}` and checking `status === "PAID"`.

<Callout type="warn">
  Don't redirect the customer back to your site based on the in-page polling result. The customer may close the tab before the redirect fires, and you'll miss the state change. Always rely on the webhook or the API.
</Callout>
